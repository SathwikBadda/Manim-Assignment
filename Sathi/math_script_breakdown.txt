# Primary Application Scenarios for the Split-Screen Solver Template

## Scenario: Translating a Visual Situation into a Computable Form

This template is best applied when a learner must move from a visible situation to a mathematical expression. The left side presents a stable model such as character interactions, items, or scenarios, while the right side converts that structure into formal arithmetic steps. The key instructional move is showing that computation emerges from real-world structure. The animation helps learners understand that algorithmic math is a direct reflection of real-world quantities and actions. This applies broadly to subtraction problems, multi-step word problems, scaling, and practical budgeting tasks across grade levels.

## Scenario: Teaching a Multi-Step Method Clearly and Sequentially

The template is highly effective when the learning objective is procedural fluency for column subtraction. The left panel acts as a persistent contextual workspace where the scenario remains visible, while the right panel introduces one calculation step at a time. Each reasoning block enters, highlights the specific column being computed, processes the math, and exits before the next begins. This makes it ideal for addition, subtraction, and multiplication algorithms. The learner experiences structure rather than clutter, and complexity feels manageable because each step is isolated and justified.

## Scenario: Managing Dynamic Galleries of Information

This structure is particularly strong when the problem involves varying numbers of inputs. The left side uses a dynamic layout system that automatically grids, arranges, and scales images and labels depending on how many items are involved in the transaction. This teaches learners that mathematical modeling applies uniformly whether there are two items or five. The left side dynamically accommodates complexity without impinging upon the isolated instructional space on the right.

## Scenario: Demonstrating Conclusion and Closure

The template supports lessons where final validation and narrative closure are as important as computation. After completing the solution, the screen transitions into a clearing phase where the arithmetic and initial visual context slide away, isolating the final result centrally. This visually separates execution from final summary, concluding realistically with a confirmatory sentence describing the result in context.

## Scenario: Building Structured Problem-Solving Habits

More broadly, this template is suited for any K–12 situation where learners must interpret, plan, execute, and confirm. The left side stabilizes the problem context; the right side manages reasoning. The entry establishes the model. The transitions manage attention. The exits prevent overload. The final consolidation reinforces narrative closure. This makes the template reusable across mathematics topics where clarity, structure, and disciplined reasoning are the primary instructional goals.

# Scene Initialization and Structural Framing

The scene opens on a dark grey background (#2C3336), creating high contrast for white, vivid green (#61D262), and orange (#EF9515) typography. The entire frame is empty initially, establishing visual calm before structural elements appear.

The title "Subtracting Money: Riya's Leftover" is constructed as a bold text block in Comic Sans MS and positioned at the top edge of the screen, centered horizontally and offset slightly downward (offset by 0.5 units). Its z-index is set above all masking layers, ensuring it remains visible throughout early transitions. The title enters via a FadeIn animation shifting upwards over 2 seconds.

Below the title, the remaining screen space is divided conceptually into a mathematical grid. A faint vertical line appears at the horizontal midpoint of the screen, dividing left and right halves. This grid line acts as a structural guide but remains visually subtle (low opacity grey). It is drawn from the bottom of the title down to the bottom of the frame via a Create animation lasting 1.5 seconds.

The left half is therefore reserved for contextual and geometric visualization (images and items).
The right half is reserved for the Column Subtraction Method and dynamic step explanations.

---

# Masking Layer Initialization

Before any right-panel content appears, a large rectangular mask matching the background color (#2C3336) is positioned over the entire left half of the screen. This mask sits above right-side panels in z-order but below the title and grid lines.

Its purpose is critical: when right-side panels slide leftward during transitions, the mask creates a “clean wipe” effect, hiding content as it crosses the vertical centerline. This ensures panels do not visually overlap into the left instructional space.

This mask remains stationary for the entire solution sequence.

# Geometry Visualization Entry (Left Quadrant)

On the left side of the split-screen, a dynamically composed gallery of visual context enters. Since the images correspond to the problem scope, they are inserted sequentially into the left half centered horizontally at x = -w/4.

1. First, the character image (Riya) is placed dynamically.
2. Second, the wallet/money bag image (representing ₹85.75) with a white label is placed below it.

The images arrange themselves based on availability, wrapping into a multi-column grid if needed to avoid overlapping. The gallery ensures the entire left half remains a structurally stable, correctly scaled visualization.

Each element of this gallery enters via a FadeIn animation shifting upwards, taking 0.5 seconds each.

At this stage, the left half is fully structured but static, while the right half remains empty.

# Right Panel Step Cycle (Sequential Instruction Flow)

The bottom portion of the right half is reserved for animated instructional panels describing the active mathematical step. Each panel is generated using dynamic wrapping to prevent horizontal text overflow and appears centered at x = w/4, near the bottom of the screen (y = -1.6).

Each step follows the same lifecycle:

1. Panel enters via Write animation.
2. A highlighted geometric interaction occurs on the math (right-top).
3. The arithmetic result of the column appears.
4. Panel pauses for processing.
5. Panel exits by sliding left, disappearing entirely behind the left masking layer.

---

# Step 1 – Subtracting the Paise

Panel 1 appears on the right, vertically positioned at -1.6. It consists of two lines:
Heading: "Step 1: Subtract the Paise!" (larger text)
Body: "75 paise - 20 paise = 55 paise" (smaller text)

The panel appears via a Write animation taking 1.725 seconds.

As the explanation focuses on the paise column:
* An orange SurroundingRectangle is created around the vertically aligned "75" and "20", indicating the target column. This rectangle triggers an Indicate animation causing it to expand and contract briefly to draw the eye over 1.425 seconds.
* Following the indication, the result text "55" fades in immediately below the horizontal line in the correct column alignment, taking 0.725 seconds.

After a short pause of 2.6 seconds, the panel shifts left rapidly (shifting LEFT by w/1.5). As it crosses the vertical centerline, the left mask hides it completely. It is then removed from the scene mentally clearing the workspace for the next instruction.

# Step 3 – Establishing the Answer

Panel 3 appears in the right half, moved slightly up to y = -1.0.
It reads: "Money Left: ₹5.55" using a bold font.

The panel appears via a Write animation lasting 1.25 seconds.

Beneath this panel, the isolated total result "₹5.55" fades into existence over 1.0 second at y = -2.5.
An orange Highlight SurroundingRectangle is drawn around this total result via a Create animation taking 0.5 seconds.
Immediately after, the total text scales up dramatically to a multiple of 1.2 via an animate.scale function to strongly emphasize mathematical closure. 

After a 4.0-second pause, Panel 3 shifts left into the mask and is removed. 

At this moment, the left half still shows the stable gallery visualization; the right half shows the completed column subtraction and a brilliantly highlighted final answer block at the bottom.

# Workspace Clearing Transition

To reset cognitive load and prepare for narrative validation, the screen divides physically:

* All computational column logic on the right—the heading, addends, lines, rectangles, and result digits—simultaneously shifts deep off the screen strictly to the RIGHT by 8 units.
* The entire gallery of images, labels, and visual setups on the left simultaneously shifts deep off the screen strictly to the LEFT by 8 units.

These animations take 0.5 seconds and completely abandon the original structures. 

The only remaining elements left in the visible scene are:
* The Title text at the very top.
* The boldly highlighted total "₹5.55" box at the bottom.

The "₹5.55" text and its orange highlight box smoothly translate upward using a concurrent animation to a central, prominent position near the top of the canvas just below the title (coordinates [0, 2.5, 0]). This takes 1.0 second.

The lower half of the screen is now completely cleared for final narrative verification.

# Final Conclusion Sequence

Below the centrally repositioned mathematical result, a new concluding horizontal banner sentence fades in shifting UP:

"Riya has ₹5.55 left!"

 The target number "₹5.55" within the sentence itself is rendered distinctly in the same bright orange (#EF9515) as the highlight box positioned directly above it. This vertically aligns the arithmetic result visually with its narrative role.

 This sentence anchors the final arithmetic validation to reality.

---

# Final Fade to Black

After allowing 2.0 seconds for digestion of the conclusion, a simultaneous FadeOut animation drops all visible elements instantly off the canvas over 1.0 second, including:
* Title
* Centered Total Result
* Total Orange Highlight Box
* Conclusion Sentence

The scene returns to the empty dark grey background, completely resetting for the next problem or segment.

# Structural Integrity and Relative Positioning Summary

Throughout the animation:

* The left half functions as a persistent visual situation that does not move until the clearing sequence.
* The upper right half remains a stable arithmetic execution environment.
* The lower right half is reserved as a transient, sequential explanatory channel.
* Panels always enter centrally within the lower right quadrant.
* Panels consistently exit sequentially leftward under a stationary obscuring mask.
* Arithmetic column alignments and highlighting precisely match panel text logic for real-time validation.
* Results always slide from calculation to central prominence to narrative sentence organically.

The scene encodes:
Context (constraint) → Arithmetic framework (process) → Column steps (execution) → Answer isolation (result) → Narrative sentence (verification).

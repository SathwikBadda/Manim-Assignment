Step 1: Anchor — The Circular Head Establishes Focus

The animation begins on a dark, neutral background with an empty frame. A solid circular shape appears first, forming the head of the map pin. The circle is introduced slightly above the vertical center of the composition, remaining horizontally centered to establish a clear focal point. It fades in cleanly as a filled shape without any outline, immediately reading as a stable and friendly marker head. Its placement implicitly reserves space below for the pointed section of the pin while anchoring the viewer’s attention to the top of the icon.

Step 2: Direction — The Pointed Base Emerges

With the circular head in place, a triangular shape appears below it to form the pin’s pointed base. The triangle is oriented downward by inversion and proportionally scaled so that its width is slightly narrower than the circle while its height extends visibly below it. The triangle is positioned by aligning its top region to overlap slightly with the lower portion of the circle, using the circle’s center as a reference rather than the frame itself. As it moves into place, the triangle visually “connects” to the circle, establishing a clear directional cue that draws the eye downward and suggests a precise location.

Step 3: Unification — The Pin Body Becomes One Form

Once both the circular head and the inverted triangle are positioned correctly, they are visually merged into a single continuous silhouette. The seam between the two shapes disappears, and the combined form reads as a unified pin body rather than separate components. The merged shape remains centered as a whole, preserving the vertical alignment established earlier. This unification step transforms the icon from a collection of primitives into a recognizable location marker symbol.

Step 4: Definition — The Inner Hole Refines the Symbol

In the final step, a smaller circular shape appears at the center of the pin’s head, aligned precisely with the center of the original outer circle. This inner circle is subtracted from the pin body, creating a hollow hole that adds visual depth and improves recognizability at small sizes. After the subtraction, the entire pin form undergoes a subtle downward adjustment as a single unit, ensuring the visual center of mass feels balanced within the frame. The icon then holds steady, rendered in solid white against the dark background, presenting a clean and universally recognizable map pin symbol.

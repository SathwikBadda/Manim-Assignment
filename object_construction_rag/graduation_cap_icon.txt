Step 1: Foundation — The Skull Cap Forms

The animation begins on a dark, near-black background with an empty frame. A short rectangular shape appears first as the foundation of the icon, introduced at the visual center of the composition and horizontally centered using the scene’s natural centering behavior. The rectangle fades in while gently scaling up, establishing itself as a stable base element. Immediately afterward, a shallow elliptical shape is positioned directly below the rectangle by aligning the ellipse’s top edge to the rectangle’s bottom edge, effectively using a relative “next-to” relationship rather than an absolute shift. This ellipse slides upward and merges seamlessly into the rectangle, forming a unified skull-cap silhouette. The combined shape remains centered as a group and is allowed to settle slightly lower than the visual midpoint of the final icon, implicitly reserving space above for the mortarboard top.

Step 2: Identity — The Mortarboard Top Locks In

With the skull cap in place, a flat diamond shape representing the mortarboard top is introduced. The diamond is horizontally aligned to the skull cap by matching their centers and is positioned just above it using a small relative buffer, as if placed with an upward “next-to” constraint. Its width is intentionally larger than the skull cap’s width, creating immediate visual hierarchy. The diamond scales into view from its own center, then performs a subtle downward corrective motion, visually locking into alignment with the base below. Once positioned, the diamond and skull cap together read as a single structured unit, with their relative spacing preserved through implicit grouping, allowing the viewer to instantly recognize the academic cap silhouette.

Step 3: Detail and Motion — The Tassel Emerges

Attention shifts to the defining detail of the cap: the tassel. A small circular knot appears precisely at the center of the diamond, positioned using the diamond’s own center point rather than the global frame. From this knot, a curved line draws itself outward toward one side of the diamond, with its control points derived relative to the diamond’s center and outer edge so that the curve naturally exits the diamond’s boundary. From the endpoint of this curve, a straight vertical line extends downward, maintaining vertical alignment and hanging freely alongside the skull cap without intersecting it. A slightly larger circular end piece appears at the bottom of this vertical line, positioned relative to the line’s endpoint, completing the tassel. The entire tassel assembly is thus constructed purely from references to existing elements, ensuring it remains correctly placed even if the cap’s proportions change.

Step 4: Completion — Final Lockup and Emphasis

In the final step, all elements of the graduation cap — the skull cap, the mortarboard top, and the tassel — are treated as a single grouped unit. This group is gently re-centered so that the overall visual weight, including the asymmetry introduced by the tassel, feels balanced within the frame. No individual element shifts relative to another; only the group’s global position is adjusted. A subtle emphasis animation, such as a slight scale-up followed by a return to the original size, is applied to the group as a whole, reinforcing completion without introducing new motion. The icon then holds steady, clean and uniformly white against the dark background, presenting a polished and fully assembled symbol of academic achievement.
